    VXP Proof

    The signed chain that records what was asked, approved, executed and read back, and how anyone verifies it.

    On this page

    • What it records

    What it records#

    Each operation produces a chain of signed statements. Each statement names the previous one by its digest, and is signed by the role that observed it (§6):

    kindSigned byWhat it records
    requestoperatorWhat was asked, and by which agent (client_id)
    termscompanyEach revision: the digest of the exact terms, the commitment (firm or conditional), the expiry, what will be read back
    counteroperatorA counter-proposal from the agent
    user_approvaloperatorApproval of the latest revision, and its method
    company_reviewcompanyA decision by a person at the company, when its rule requires one
    executionoperatorThe single execution, on the approved revision
    read_backoperatorWhat the company's system showed afterwards, term by term
    outcomecompanysucceeded, failed, cancelled, expired or unknown
    correction, revocationcompanyWhat became known after the final state

    The rules that make it worth something#

    • Binding by digest (P1): approval, execution and outcome all cite the digest of the exact revision. Terms that change after approval no longer match.
    • No success without reading the system (P6, O7): a capability that cannot be read back never signs succeeded.
    • Unknown is signed, not hidden (P3): if it is not known whether the effect happened, the company signs unknown, and a later fact goes in as a correction (P4).
    • Contradictions stop (P5): if the system shows something other than what was approved, the read-back says so, the result stays unknown, and nothing is retried.
    • The verifier derives the level (P7): the issuer never declares how strong its own evidence is.

    Formats#

    • One SD-JWT per statement (RFC 9901), ES256, header typ: vxp-stmt+sd-jwt and kid = RFC 7638 thumbprint.
    • Payload in JCS (RFC 8785). Personal data travels only as selective disclosures.
    • The trust members, meaning whose keys these are, where they are published, who holds the company's key and which log is used, are published in the issuer's RFC 8414 metadata as vxp_proof, never inside the package.

    Details: §6, format, §6, chain rules.

    Fetching a proof#

    In the native profile, the agent fetches the package of its own request:

    Fetch the proofhttp
    GET /v1/requests/{request_id}/proof
Authorization: DPoP <token>
DPoP: <proof>

    The package is {v, company, op, statements[], log}. Someone else's request and a missing one answer the same way: 404 request_not_found.

    Read it with the table in §10, reading a verdict:

    • valid is integrity;
    • company, anchor and key_source are provenance;
    • approval is the method of approval;
    • final_state is what the company asserts;
    • derived_assurance, single_root and logged are how much supports it.

    Here single_root: operator and logged: false: an attestation signed by the operator, intact and ordered, not an independent confirmation.

    Keys and time#

    Proofs are only as durable as the keys that verify them. Keep proof keys persistent. When you rotate, keep the earlier public key published for as long as proofs signed with it are to be verified (§10, key rotation). A proof whose key was retired still verifies against public material saved while that key was published.
