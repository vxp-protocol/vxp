    Guide for companies

    How to make a company's existing systems speak VXP with the native profile, step by step, and how to prove it.

    On this page

    • What you build
    • 1. Decide what you offer
    • 2. Publish discovery
    • 3. Identify agents and link accounts
    • 4. Hold requests until there is authority
    • 5. Propose exact terms
    • 6. Execute once, then read back
    • 7. Keep your keys
    • 8. Optionally, a relationship
    • 9. Prove it
    • Running the reference implementation as your starting point

    This guide adds the company side of VXP to systems you already run: an order system, a booking system, a claims queue. You do not need SOVRAS, Poppy or PACT. Each step names the rules it implements; the specification is the exact text.

    What you build#

    PieceWhat it doesRules
    DiscoveryPublish /.well-known/vxp.json and your issuer's RFC 8414 metadata[[native-discovery§8 discovery]]
    Authorization serverClients with private_key_jwt, DPoP-bound tokens, device authorization with RAR[[native-authorization§8 authorization]], ID1–ID3
    The /v1 endpointsRequests, proposals, acceptance, cancellation, events, proof[[native-endpoints§8 endpoints]]
    Your controlsThe operation requirements, enforced in front of your systemsO1–O7
    VXP Proof issuerSign the chain, publish your keys, keep them persistent[[proof§6]], [[key-rotation§10 keys]]
    VXP Relationship (optional)Agreements, signed outbound requests, answers[[relationship§7]]

    The reference implementation in reference/ is one complete example, with every piece above. It is reference code, not a product: copy what helps you, and keep your own systems as the source of truth.

    1. Decide what you offer#

    List the operations an external agent may ask for. For each one, write down:

    • what it reads or changes;
    • whether a person must approve each revision;
    • what you can read back afterwards to confirm it happened.

    An operation you cannot read back can never be declared succeeded (P6). Publish only those operations in vxp.json capabilities, each with access (public or delegated) and admission (static or dynamic).

    2. Publish discovery#

    Serve vxp.json on your own domain. The issuer it names must serve RFC 8414 metadata with an identical issuer. Put the proof trust members (vxp_proof: your key set, the operator's, the log and the custody) only in that metadata, and list your discovery domain in vxp_proof.domains. A verifier anchors your keys there, never in a document handed over with a package (§10 anchoring).

    3. Identify agents and link accounts#

    • Accept clients identified by a Client ID Metadata Document. Authenticate them with private_key_jwt, and issue DPoP-bound tokens.
    • Link a customer account to an agent only through an authentication you control, for example a one-time code over a channel you already have for that customer, and keep the evidence. Never link by a matching name, email or phone number (ID1). An identifier shared by two accounts links neither (ID2).
    • The owner of an operation is (profile, client_id, sub, account). Nobody else sees it (ID3, O1).

    4. Hold requests until there is authority#

    When an agent asks without authority, answer 202 authorization_required with exactly the authorization_details that are missing (O2). Your consent screen shows each item in business language, and the person approves on your page (R3). Re-evaluate the waiting request when the agent repeats it with the same Idempotency-Key (§8 idempotency).

    5. Propose exact terms#

    Turn the request into a revision with exact terms, a fresh vxp_salt, an expiry and a commitment:

    • firm, when only your systems are involved;
    • conditional, listing what depends on a third party (§8 commitment).

    Sign it into the proof (P1, O4). A change of terms is a new revision. Accepting an older one gets 409 terms_changed.

    6. Execute once, then read back#

    On acceptance:

    1. Re-check authority, the policy and the precondition.
    2. Execute once per resource, even if the same request arrives by several paths (O6, I2).
    3. Read your system back.
    4. Only if what you read matches the terms, sign succeeded (O7).

    If you cannot tell whether it happened, sign unknown and correct it later (P3, P4). If what you read contradicts the terms, sign that, keep unknown and send it to a person (P5). When your rule requires it, a person at the company decides on the same digest before execution (O5).

    7. Keep your keys#

    Generate proof keys once and keep them; publish earlier public keys after a rotation; never reuse a retired key (§10 key rotation). Proofs signed with keys you lose can no longer be checked by anyone.

    8. Optionally, a relationship#

    If you want to reach agents later, publish vxp_relationship (its own key set and your company_initiated_actions), accept the relationship RAR in the same consent, and follow R1–R17. See VXP Relationship.

    9. Prove it#

    Run the conformance validator against your deployment. It acts as a personal agent and reports every check with the rule it tests:

    Validate your implementationsh
    node conformance/validate.ts my-config.json --report my-report.json

    Then go through conformance/CHECKLIST.md for what no tool can see from outside. See Tests and conformance.

    Running the reference implementation as your starting point#

    The reference company, native profile onlysh
    cd reference
VXP_ADAPTERS=none VXP_KEYS_DIR=./.keys VXP_SHOW_CHANNEL=1 npm start

    It serves three fictitious test companies on http://localhost:3100. The store's discovery is at /.well-known/vxp.json. VXP_SHOW_CHANNEL=1 prints to the console what the company would send through its channel, such as the account-linking code, because that channel is simulated.
